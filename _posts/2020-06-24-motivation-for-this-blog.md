### Firstly, why blog?

Reading and learning from blogs has been an edifying experience for me. Learning from videos or courses is time consuming as they address a large audience and you might find yourself spending most of your time watching content you already have knowledge of. Official documentation for most of the softwares are great but they can be very expound at times. Blogs, according me to are usually to-the-point and provide you with the exact information you are looking for.

Having learned copious amount of things just by reading blogs, I thought of sharing my experiences on the internet to help people out.

Moreover, everytime I learn something new, I like to ask myself if I really understand it or not. And, the best way to know if I truely understand something is by exploring my ability to explain it to someone else in a concise manner. A blog post is a great way to do that. Organizing knowledge always helps me synthesize my own ideas.

### What motivates me to write blogs? 

I think different people have different motivations for blogging. These are mine!

* Like I mentioned in the previous answer, writing helps me understand the concepts better. I cross verify the information in my blogs 10 times before posting it, just to make sure I do not share the wrong information. This helps in polishing the concepts.

* I am very ebullient about learning new things which are not commonly done by others. According to me this is what differentiates developers. Taking an "all-in-one" crash course is hardly useful as it mearly takes you through the basics. Whenever you try something new, which is not pursued by the vast majority, you may find yourself reading elusive and tedious documentations. I often find blog posts from developers very helpful in this situation. Knowing how conducive blogs can be at times, I get motivated to share my knowledge whenever I try something new. 

* There have been numerous occassions where I have spent a lot of time trying to solve a problem but it took barely five seconds before my colleague asked me a simple question that completely stumped me - why hadn’t I thought of that?! Well, it’s simply because of biases. We all look at the world in a certain way and get attuned to looking at it the same way which makes it difficult to break out of it. We might not find it as easy to find bugs in our code as easily as someone who is looking at it with a fresh perspective. I want to share my 2 cents and my take on different things through this blog. Maybe this "fresh" perspective would be helpful to someone out there.

* As Rachel Thomas rightly said in one of her blogs, 
> You are best positioned to help people one step behind you. The material is still fresh in your mind. Many experts have forgotten what it was like to be a beginner (or an intermediate) and have forgotten why the topic is hard to understand when you first hear it. The context of your particular background, your particular style, and your knowledge level will give a different twist to what you’re writing about.

* As an undergraduate student, I always look forward to solicit advice from someone who has been through the same path before and has experienced it beforehand. I keep calling my peers and seniors for advise or simply thoughts on a certain set of subjects currently revolving around academics, finding internships, jobs, conducting research, undergraduate studies, surviving engineering, exploring new fields in Computer Science, GRE, and what not. Having been through some of these problems, I want to tell people things that I wish I knew a year ago!

* One of my idols, Arnold Schwarzenegger, in his famous speech [6 Rules of Success](https://open.spotify.com/playlist/2ysRYavPjBjJ3QwVMzgBGb?si=jnf4VWpmT5iHzMj24uaYLA){:target="_blank" rel="noopener"} says: 
>We are not where we are today without any help. We all get help along the way. We've never done it by ourselves. Everything I have done—show business, politics, money I made, success in business and bodybuilding, my foundation, environmental organisations—it's always with a lot of help from a lot of people. Since they helped me, I should now help others! This is why I have after-school programs, why I help with Special Olympics, and why I care about environmental issues. I want to clean up the environment and create a green-energy future so our world is a better place than when we inherited it.
We have to help people. There are millions of children who need help, who come from families that don't have enough money or don't read well. Help them learn to read! Encourage people to exercise! Join an organisation where you can give a kid a hug, hang a medal around their neck and say, "You're a winner! We believe in you. We love you. We care for you."
Do something for your community, your state, your country. Give something back.

Almost everything I have learned was through the content created by the developers who were kind enough to share their knowledge on the web. Through this blog, I want to share the limited knowledge I have acquired and give something back to the community.


### What are some of the blogs I enjoy reading? 

[Rachel Thomas](https://medium.com/@racheltho){:target="_blank" rel="noopener"} has motivated me to start this blog. Her post "[Why you (yes,you) should blog](https://medium.com/@racheltho/why-you-yes-you-should-blog-7d2544ac1045){:target="_blank" rel="noopener"}" was very conducive in making up my mind. I highly recommend you to read it.

I'm fascinated by [Anish Athalye's research](https://www.anish.io/){:target="_blank" rel="noopener"} and his blogs explaining those. I have read many of this published papers start to end (however, I understood only half of the content :p). One of my favourites and most helpful courses I ever took was instructed my him. It is called [The Missing Semester of Your CS Education](https://missing.csail.mit.edu/){:target="_blank" rel="noopener"} which teaches one critical subject that’s rarely covered, and is instead left to students to figure out on their own: proficiency with their tools. It teachs you how to master the command-line, use a powerful text editor, use fancy features of version control systems, and much more!

[Swapneel Mehta's journey](https://swapneelm.github.io/cern-openlab-internship){:target="_blank" rel="noopener"} of getting into CERN has been one of my favourite articles, mainly because he is one of my seniors who had an exciting journey, from a tier 3 engineering college to the largest particle physics laboratory in the world and I look forward to having a similar one.
